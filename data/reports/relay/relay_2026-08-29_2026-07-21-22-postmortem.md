# Post-Mortem — the 2026-07-21/22 Event and Its 5-Week Blind Spot

**Written:** 2026-08-29. Covers everything this session (and the OPS TRIAGE /
options-gap work earlier today) traced back to the week of 2026-07-21.
Root-cause work on the live fleet-emission piece is **partially blocked on
a sudo-gated fix and one open Admiral decision** — see "Status of the P0
fix" below. This doc is the full story regardless, per directive.

## What actually happened (single timeline)

**2026-07-21, day:** GEX collector dies (root-caused in this session's
earlier OPS TRIAGE-adjacent work: 07-21 stand-down rename +
never-reinstalled crontab). `mlx-qwen3` — a separately-run local MLX
inference server on port 8899, with **no launchd or cron supervision at
all** — stops emitting signals at 02:30 the next day (last row

**Correction on `mlx-qwen3` timing:** its last signal was **2026-07-18**,
four days *before* the 07-21/22 event, not caused by it. It is a fully
separate, independent death with no supervision to have ever caught it —
included here because it's part of the same "signal emission collapsed
and nobody noticed" pattern, not because it shares the 07-21/22 root
cause.

**2026-07-22, after market close:** Captain Kirk gives an explicit,
deliberate **final stand-down order** for the entire fleet — this is
documented, not reconstructed: six seasons, 73 agents, 1,569+ trades,
RULE #1 never violated, fleet "at permanent anchor." The session executing
the order:
- Kills `main.py` (trader), `watchdog.py`, `signal-center`.
- Disables 22 `com.ollietrades.*`/`com.trademinds.*` launchd jobs
  (`launchctl disable` + `bootout`).
- Renames 23 scripts to `*.quietdown-disabled-2026-07-22` (crontab itself
  was unwritable — a pre-existing EINTR bug — so renaming was the
  reversible workaround instead of editing the crontab text).
- Source-health watcher and GEX collector are two of those 23 scripts —
  their deaths on 07-21/22 (found separately in OPS TRIAGE earlier today)
  are not a coincidence or a bug: they're this same deliberate action.

**A reboot was planned but never executed** (per the same session's own
follow-up note) — `last reboot` still showed 2026-07-13, a week *before*
the stand-down, when the verification pass ran later that same day. The
fleet was stopped without a reboot; the "still owed" reversal checklist
from that session was never completed by anyone until pieces of it got
picked up ad hoc over the following five weeks.

**2026-07-23 → 2026-08-24, 33 days:** Fleet-wide silence. Confirmed two
independent ways: `logs/trader.log` has **zero lines** for the entire
window, and the `trades` table shows **zero executions from any of ~78
active agents** for the entire window. This isn't a bug during this
window — it's the stand-down simply continuing, uninterrupted, for far
longer than "permanent anchor" was probably meant to mean in practice.

**~2026-08-24:** `main.py` comes back up (exact trigger/session not
identified in this pass — not investigated further, out of scope for
"root-cause the emission outage"). Trading resumes, but **only main.py
itself was restarted** — the 22 disabled launchd jobs and 23 renamed
scripts were not systematically reversed.

**2026-08-27:** A companion session restores `com.trademinds.signal-center`
specifically (one job off the 22-job disabled list) and, while checking
the full `launchctl print-disabled` list, finds ~23 *more* jobs still
disabled from 07-22. Rather than reverse them all, it correctly stops and
files `QUESTION_fleet-standdown-reversal.md` — **still open, unanswered,
as of this writing.**

**2026-08-27 07:18, same day:** In an unrelated thread (the "Ollama Model
Aliases" investigation — `plutus-v1`/`ministral-3:3b`/`qwen3:4b` all found
to be aliases of the same `qwen3:8b` weights), `/Library/LaunchDaemons/
com.ollama.serve.plist` is edited: adds `OLLAMA_MAX_LOADED_MODELS=1`,
`OLLAMA_FLASH_ATTENTION=1`, `OLLAMA_KV_CACHE_TYPE=q8_0`. **This
introduces a second, independent regression on top of the first**: the
two agents that account for nearly all live signal volume
(`ollama-plutus`, `ollama-qwen3`) go from a median 6-8s gap between
signals to 86-91s — confirmed via a clean before/after split at the exact
plist-edit timestamp, not a guess. Empirically ruled out model-swap
thrashing between the aliased tags (a live A/B `curl` test shows Ollama
correctly dedupes by underlying digest — switching between `qwen3:8b` and
`plutus-v1` tags costs ~0.75s, not a reload). Leading suspect is
`OLLAMA_FLASH_ATTENTION`/`OLLAMA_KV_CACHE_TYPE` slowing raw inference
rather than the load-count cap — not yet confirmed with an A/B revert
(blocked on sudo, see below).

**2026-08-27 – 2026-08-29 (today):** Trader restarts logged at 08-27
10:42 and 08-28 20:08 (crash/keepalive-recovery pattern, not
investigated further this pass), current process uptime only 79 minutes

`ollama-qwen3`, `capitol-trades`) have emitted *any* `signals` row since
recovery — checked against the pre-outage baseline (mid-June through

emitter, `mlx-qwen3` (4,878 signals in the 9 days before its own 07-18

**2026-08-29, today:** OPS TRIAGE (this session, earlier) finds and fixes
the GEX collector and source-health watcher — 2 of the 23 renamed

`check_cron_missing_scripts` in `hm_ops_sentinel.py`, which — as a side
effect — now correctly flags the other ~14 still-renamed scripts as
broken cron entries (it doesn't know or care *why* they're broken, it
just tells the truth about crontab). The options-gap investigation
(this session, later) traces the wheel/CSP-shadow dormancy to the same

## Shared root cause vs. independent chain

Not one root cause — **two**, layered:

1. **The 33-day fleet-wide dark period** — root cause is a deliberate,
   documented Admiral order, not a bug. What *is* a defect is that the
   order's own "still owed" reversal checklist was never completed, so
   the fleet came back on 08-24 in a half-stood-down state that nobody
   had fully audited until this week.
2. **The 08-27 Ollama-plist regression** — a genuine, accidental
   side-effect of unrelated tuning work, layered on top of #1's
   incomplete recovery. This one has no deliberate justification and
   should just be fixed/reverted.

`mlx-qwen3`'s death (07-18) is a third, fully independent thread — not
caused by either of the above, just never supervised in the first place.

## Why nothing alerted for 5+ weeks

`hm_ops_sentinel.py` (the only ops-health watchdog on this machine) ran
this whole time — but its check roster, as it existed before today, was:
`check_fd_count`, `check_lock_errors`, `check_signals_v2_queue`,
`check_riker_heartbeat` (retired, no-op). **None of those check whether
the trader process itself is running, whether it's emitting signals, or
whether Ollama inference is slow.** The sentinel watched for a handful of
specific symptoms (fd leaks, lock errors, one specific queue) inside a
process it never confirmed was alive. A dead-but-not-crashed trader — or
a live-but-crawling one — produced literally nothing for any of these
checks to fire on. Today's OPS TRIAGE added 3 more checks
(`check_collector_freshness`, `check_status_page_heartbeat`,
`check_source_health_watcher_heartbeat`, `check_cron_missing_scripts` —
4, actually), which is real, meaningful progress, but every one of them
is still a *symptom* check (a specific heartbeat file, a specific queue,
a specific cron log) rather than a *process-alive* or *throughput* check.

## The "still wouldn't catch" list — explicit backlog

None of today's fixes would have caught, and **still would not catch**,
any of the following. Filing these as concrete backlog items, not vague
concerns:

1. **No trader-process-alive check.** Nothing in `hm_ops_sentinel.py`
   confirms `main.py` is running at all, independent of any specific
   symptom. A full process death (or a stand-down that never gets
   reversed) produces zero alerts today, exactly as it did for 33 days.
   *Proposed:* a heartbeat the trader itself writes every scan cycle,
   checked by the sentinel on a different mechanism (per the standing
   "alarm must not share a failure mode with what it watches" doctrine).

2. **No signal-emission-rate check.** Nothing watches `signals` table
   volume or per-agent cadence. The 08-27 Ollama-plist regression (a
   ~15x per-agent slowdown) produced zero alerts and would still produce
   zero today. *Proposed:* a rolling-window check on median inter-signal
   gap per active LLM-backed agent, alerting on a large jump versus its
   own trailing baseline (not a fixed threshold — different agents have
   different natural cadences).

3. **No launchd-disabled-job audit — CLOSED this session.**
   `check_cron_missing_scripts` only reads `crontab -l`; it had no
   visibility into `launchctl print-disabled`. Fixed:
   `check_launchd_jobs_health` (new in `hm_ops_sentinel.py`) covers all
   18 jobs reactivated today — flags drift back into the disabled state,
   and flags staleness (enabled+loaded but silently stopped firing)
   against a cadence-scaled ceiling per job. Full disposition + the 5
   jobs deliberately left out of this coverage (3 pre-dead, 1
   code-retired, 1 orphan): `docs/HM-STANDDOWN-SUCCESSOR-2026-08-29.md`.

4. **No unsupervised-service inventory.** `mlx-qwen3`'s server has never
   had a launchd plist or crontab entry — it isn't just currently broken,
   it was never watchable by anything in this codebase. There may be
   other services in the same state; nobody has enumerated "everything
   the fleet depends on" against "everything that's actually supervised."
   *Proposed:* an explicit inventory pass (grep every `:PORT` / `localhost`
   dependency the fleet code calls, cross-reference against launchd +
   crontab), not a code fix — a one-time audit task.

5. **No inference-latency check.** Nothing measures Ollama call latency
   directly. The `[SCAN-SUBCALL]` timing lines exist in `ai_brain.py` and
   *could* be aggregated, but nothing currently reads them for alerting
   purposes. *Proposed:* surface `ollama_batch`/model-call wall time as a
   sentinel metric, not just a scan-log line nobody watches live.

6. **No weekly_tuning_crew heartbeat.** Its last `audition_proposed` row
   is 2026-07-19 — six weeks silent, presumably another casualty of the
   same window, not separately confirmed in this pass. Not currently
   covered by any heartbeat check.
