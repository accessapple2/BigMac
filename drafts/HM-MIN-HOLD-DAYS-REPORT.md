## Method

The fleet sweep (2026-07-04) flagged `MIN_HOLD_DAYS` as the dominant driver behind the rule-
consolidation's return jump: ollama-qwen3 +20.06%→+26.07%, McCoy (ollama-plutus) +13.74%→+21.67%,
Spock (deepseek-7b-grok4) +0.56%→+5.00%. This report quantifies that claim directly: a controlled
sweep across `MIN_HOLD_DAYS ∈ {0, 1, 2, 3, 5, 8}`, holding everything else fixed (same guardrails,
same realism flags `ENFORCE_DISPATCH_REALISM`/`ENFORCE_COST_MODEL`, same signal history per agent),
on the three named agents.

**Robustness check (the actual point of this report, not just the winner):** each agent's full
signal-history window was split at its midpoint into two independent, non-overlapping halves, and
the same sweep re-run on each half. A hold value that wins the full window but doesn't reproduce in
either half independently is a red flag for overfitting to that specific continuous window's trade
timing, not evidence of a real structural edge — this is exactly the "relative_strength trap" the
directive asked to guard against.

## Robustness verdict: **config-fragile, not a settled universal winner**

- **The one truly robust finding:** hold=0 is unambiguously worse than any positive minimum hold,
  for every agent, in every window and sub-window, with no exception. This alone supports "some
  minimum hold beats none" — which is exactly what the already-shipped flat `MIN_HOLD_DAYS=3`
  represents (live-parity motivated, not curve-fit).
- **Beyond hold=0, no single value dominates across agents AND survives the split-half check.**
  ollama-qwen3's winner is ambiguous between 3/5/8. McCoy's full-window "3" is contradicted by both
  of its own halves (2 wins first half, 8 wins second half, 3 wins neither). Spock's "5" reproduces
  once (first half) but is irrelevant in a regime where the second half is negative regardless of
  hold value.
- This is the relative_strength trap in action: a single continuous-window sweep produced an
  attractive-looking "winner" per agent, but decomposing the same data into two honest, independent
  halves shows the winner is not stable — most acutely for McCoy, whose reported +21.72% headline
  is not a number this data actually supports out of sample.

## Recommendation: **hold at current (flat MIN_HOLD_DAYS=3), do not graduate a tuned/per-agent value**

- **Do not graduate** any per-agent or higher hold value (5, 8) to live based on this sweep — the
  apparent gains beyond hold=0 are not robust enough across the split-half check to trust, and
  doing so risks curve-fitting to this specific historical window (exactly what happened to McCoy's
  headline number here).
- **Keep the already-shipped flat `MIN_HOLD_DAYS=3`** as-is. It is defensible on a different basis
  than this sweep: it matches live `paper_trader._check_min_hold()`'s 72h/3-day SWING parity
  (HM-AGENT-RULES-CONSOLIDATION doctrine), not a backtest-optimized value — which is actually a
  *better* justification than anything this sweep could produce, since it's grounded in matching
  live behavior rather than in-sample fitting.
- **If further tuning is wanted:** shadow-monitor forward (out-of-sample, not backtested) performance
  at the current hold=3 vs one alternative candidate (hold=5, the only value with any cross-window
  consistency at all, and only for Spock) for a real forward window before considering any live
  change. Do not act on the backtest numbers in this report alone.
- **Regime caveat applies to all of this:** every agent's second-half window sits inside the
  post-2026-06-11 `CAUTIOUS_BEAR` regime (see `drafts/HM-TROI-DEEPDIVE-REPORT.md`). Any future sweep
  should stratify by regime, not just split by calendar midpoint, to separate "does hold-duration
  matter" from "does the regime matter" -- this report's split-half method conflates the two for
  McCoy and Spock, both of whose second halves happen to fall mostly in the bear window.
